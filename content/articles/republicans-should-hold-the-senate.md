Tomorrow is the long awaited Mid-term elections. All 435 House seats and 35 of the 100 Senate seats will be up for grabs. The Republicans currently hold a majority in both parts of the legislative branch of government, but a Blue Wave is expected. Frequently, the party in power loses momentum from their election win two years prior, and the pendulum swings in the other direction. Once again, many polls and betting markets sense a Blue Tsunami is approaching. According to Polymarket, the Democrats will take control of the Senate, as they would gain six seats to increase their total to 53. However, the polls have been wildly inaccurate during the recent elections, meaning this outcome is dubious. The Democrats need to gain a minimum of four seats to hold a majority in the Senate, whilst continuing to win in states like Michigan, places where they already have those seats. The following states are the plausible places where the Democrats aim to flip: Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Kansas, Maine and Alaska. The issue that many pollsters seem to have is that they omit the results of the 2024 election. All of the states above, with the exception of Maine, voted for Donald Trump in the Presidential election 2 years ago. A state with one of the biggest margins for Trump in 2024 now finds itself in a closely contested Senate race and is likely going to be the state that decides who obtains the majority in the Senate: Texas.

Despite taking Texas by over 13 points, James Talarico has a fighting chance of becoming the first Democratic Senator in Texas in decades. James Talarico is a White male who has frequently opened up about his Christian Faith. As this is a large demographic in Texas, the entire pitch for Talarico is that he is a regular Texan, whilst his opponent, Ken Paxton, is a mess, caught up in endless controversies. However, after examining what kind of a person Talarico truly is, one could recognise his inability to stick to one position. Talarico describes himself as a  “Christian who hates Christianity.” And frequently compares the teachings of Jesus to Muslim philosophers, further uncovering his charade. In an interview with Fox News, Talarico was questioned on his previous remarks on there being six sexes, to which he responded, “There are two.” When the reporter pushed further on his earlier statements about God being “Non-binary”, Talarico defended his behaviour, simply referring to it as provocative. When asked about voting for a national ban on transgender surgeries of minors, he answered, “Absolutely”, which contradicts his earlier voting in 2023, when Talarico voted against Texas Senate Bill 14, which banned exactly that. Even those closest to Talarico, such as his local pastor, believe that Talarico is straying from his faith and identity. These incidents show that James Talarico does not say what he believes, which makes him unlikely to win. If Colin Allred, the highly popular NFL star who ran in 2024, lost badly to Ted Cruz, then Talarico is unlikely to pull it off in Texas. Another close race is in Maine.

Unlike the other close races, Maine did not vote for Trump in 2024. The Democratic politician who won the primary with 76% of the vote was a man named Graham Platner. However, Platner withdrew after allegations of sexual misconduct, as well as the revelation of his Totenkopf tattoo, a skull that was used as a prominent Nazi symbol. Susan Collins is likely to win, as she had a strong debate performance, the Democrats had many changes, and she is a fantastic politician who has been a part of the Senate for multiple decades.

Ann Seltzer, a well-known pollster, delivered a shocking poll before the 2024 election, in which she claimed Iowa would be won by the Democrats by 3 points. She was off by 16. Despite this, the Democrats have a strong chance of taking Iowa. The incumbent Republican, Joni Ernst, retired, and the new Republican candidate is Ashley Hinson, who is a three-term congresswoman and was endorsed by Trump. The man who is running for office for the Democrats is the highly successful Paralympian, Josh Tarek. The polls are showing that a tight race will emerge. Suffolk says Tarek is up by four, whilst the most recent poll by Insider Advantage says Hinson leads by two. In 2022, Tarek ran for a republican leaning house seat and won by six votes after a recount. In 2024, he held it by five points in a district that Donald Trump eventually carried. It is important to see what he actually did with the office he already has, as this is the only evidence of what he will do with the one he wants. Between January and May of this year, he has missed 261 floor votes, which was more than half. In an interview with John Karl from the ABC, Tarek described himself as a “Prairie Populist”, “Proud Moderate” and “Common Sense Candidate”, all of which are common phrases Tarek uses to describe himself. When Karl asked a follow-up question about whether the Democratic Party has moved too far to the left (which a majority of Iowans told the New York Times it has), Tarek’s answer was, “I don’t think a lot about the party as a whole; I think about myself, and I think about Iowa.” Tarek doesn’t need to think about the party because the party has already done the thinking for him. Tarek is a rubber stamp. During his debate, he attacked Donald Trump’s policies without mentioning Trump’s name. That seems more like a candidate who was coached very carefully not to say anything that would go viral and possibly denigrate the party, than someone who primarily thinks about himself and his state. Tarek is a conventional, national Democrat who has an astounding personal story. However, this story is doing all the work for him, as it is relayed in every campaign ad, and it is why 16% of Iowans told Suffolk they do not have a confirmed opinion on Tarek. If the Democrats are unlikely to take these seats, then they must hold on to the seats they already have. However, even those races are uncertain, as it is likely the Democrats will lose Michigan.

These races indicate that the Republicans will likely have a successful election. However, the polls could still hint at the Democrats taking some more battleground areas. Ohio is unlikely to go blue, as Bernie Moreno flipped the state red in 2024 by beating the incumbent Sherrod Brown by four points. However, the situation in Alaska is highly questionable, and it will likely result in a Democratic victory. The race in North Carolina is a walkaway, despite Trump winning that state in 2024. However, the Democrats need to gain at least four, without losing any, so two more seats are needed. Kansas will likely be kept by Republicans, which gives the Democrats less successful outcomes.

To summarise the predictions made, the Democrats will gain Alaska and North Carolina, but lose Michigan, meaning the Republicans still have a majority, as they would have 52. While it is clear that this article has predicted an optimistic outcome for the republicans (which usually is an oxymoron), there is a chance all of the states mentioned will go blue. These races are listed as toss-ups, but due to common abnormalities in polling, it is reasonable to assume that the pollsters are once again undervaluing the Republican turnout, which would mean, against all odds, the Republicans should hold the Senate. This election cycle, the Democrats needed a strong set of politicians with an even stronger set of values to overtake the Republican Government. Instead, they have candidates who are too extreme or change their position when it is convenient to them. The lack of integral candidates, especially in the places where there are tight races, will subsequently cause a loss of trust, which might cost the Democrats their chance of taking the Senate.
